Six ideas to keep

  1. A trade with no skill is a fair bet. Stop S and target T win with probability S ÷ (S + T). Real data agrees to about one percentage point.
  2. Your pass chance is fixed by the rules. It equals distance to floor ÷ (distance to floor + distance to target), whatever the sizing.
  3. The value is the loss allowance. In a fair game, the profits withdrawn over a funded account's life average the allowance the firm lets you lose; you keep the split.
  4. Time is the second half of the answer. Trades per phase fall with the square of risk per trade, and time per trade rises with the square of the stop. Higher reward-to-risk cuts costs without adding time.
  5. Choose the instrument by movement per unit of cost, not by trading hours or leverage. The Nasdaq CFD wins at FTMO. Crypto loses despite trading 24/7.
  6. The counterparty is the real risk. Payouts are discretionary and firms can fail. Part 2 stays inside the firm's risk guidance and cannot remove that risk.
