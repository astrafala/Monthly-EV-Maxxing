Chapter 4

The bracket trade

Every trade in the plan is a bracket: one position with a stop-loss and a take-profit attached the moment it opens, never moved, and closed at the latest at 20:00 UTC the same day.

4.1 Set-up

Open a long position at price \(e\) with notional \(N\) dollars (a short is the mirror image), stop a fraction \(s\) below the entry and target a fraction \(u\) above. The plan fixes three things and derives the rest:

  • the risk \(l\): what a stopped trade loses before costs. Normally \(l = L\) (1–1.75% of the account, searched in Chapter 9), but never more than the room left, net of the trade's own cost (5.4);
  • the stop distance \(s = m\sigma\): \(m\) hourly standard deviations;
  • the win \(w\) the trade nets after costs if it reaches its target: \(w = \min\big(\max(\min(k\,l,\ \text{distance to the target}),\ w_{\min}),\ \text{caps}\big)\), where \(k\) is the reward-to-risk setting, the caps are the plan's 40% concentration policy (6.7) and any firm cap, and \(w_{\min} = l\,(0.6/m - \rho)\) keeps the take-profit at least 0.6 hourly standard deviations from the entry (a target closer than that is reached a little past it). If a cap leaves less than \(w_{\min}\): with nothing made yet that day, the risk is lowered until the cap is a full minimum-distance win (a small payout target, Maven's ceiling), never below the smallest position; with profit already made that day, no further trade until tomorrow.
  • the volume: \(l/s\) divided by the value of one lot, rounded down to the volume step (0.01 lot of a CFD, one futures contract), never below the smallest volume (10.1). The trade's risk, cost and margin are those of the rounded volume; a win set by a cap (the target, a rule) keeps its amount, its take-profit placed for exactly that win, while an uncapped win \(k\,l\) scales with the rounded risk.
  • the breach level: the loss, cost included, at which the account breaches (the nearer of its floor and today's loss limit). It is a barrier of every trade: if the price reaches it, the account ends there, whatever the price does afterwards. A normal trade's stop is well inside it (5.4), so it matters only for the last trade near the floor and during a holding time (6.3, 7.4).
Position and target $$N = \frac{l}{s}, \qquad N u - c = w \;\Rightarrow\; u = \frac{s\,(w+c)}{l}.$$

A win pays \(+w\) and a loss costs \(-(l+c)\), where \(c = \kappa N\) is the round-trip cost (spread, commission, slippage allowance) of this position. (With whole lots, \(N\) is the rounded volume's notional and \(l = Ns\); the formulas below hold for it.)

4.2 The win probability

The price is a martingale and the trade ends at the first touch of the stop or the target. If nothing else can end it and fills are exact, the exit price is bounded, so optional stopping (2.4) gives \(p\,e(1+u) + (1-p)\,e(1-s) = e\), i.e. \(p = s/(s+u)\):

Win probability of a zero-skill bracket (fixed cost, exact fills) $$p = \frac{l}{l + w + c} = \frac{1}{1 + w/l + c/l}.$$

4.3 The mean and the variance of one trade

With outcomes \(+w\) (probability \(p\)) and \(-(l+c)\):

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{pnl}] = p\,w - (1-p)(l+c) = \frac{l\,w - (w+c)(l+c)}{l+w+c} = -c,$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(\text{pnl}) = p\,(w+c)^2 + (1-p)\,l^2 = l\,(w+c).$$

These hold for the binary trade of 4.2 with a cost known at entry. Some outcomes in the plan are not binary. A trade still open at 20:00 UTC is closed at that hour's price, somewhere between stop and target; a filler trade (6.3) is closed after 3 minutes; at Blue Guardian a position whose price is already beyond its stop or target when its 3-minute hold ends is closed there (7.4). In each case the price is still a martingale and the closing time a bounded stopping time, so the mean is still \(-c\), but the variance and the distribution differ. And a price that gaps through a level fills at the gap. The engine simulates all of them; the chain of Chapter 5 uses the binary trade.

4.4 What a trade costs

The round-trip cost is a fraction \(\kappa\) of the notional, so per dollar of risk

$$\rho \equiv \frac{c}{l} = \frac{\kappa N}{l} = \frac{\kappa}{m\sigma}.$$ [[COST_TABLE]]

Version 6 held winning trades through nights and paid overnight financing \(\varphi N\) per rollover, a cost that is random (it depends on how long the trade lasts) and was not in the formulas. Versions 7 and 8 hold no position through a daily reset (6.5), so there is no financing to add, and \(\rho\) is the whole cost.

4.6 Ways a simulation can flatter a trade

  1. Skipping the entry bar. Checking levels only from the next hour ignores stop-outs inside the entry hour. (Measured in version 4: +0.14 R per trade, all fake.)
  2. Sampling prices too rarely. A few prices an hour miss touches in between, and the nearer level (the stop) is missed more often. The bridge extremes of 3.3 remove it.
  3. Ties inside one bar. When one bar's range contains both levels, the bar alone does not say which came first. Version 5 used the fair chance; versions 6 and 7 replay five-minute sub-steps; version 8 replays one-minute sub-steps, and the remaining error is bounded in 3.3.
  4. Gaps. A price that opens beyond the stop fills at the open. Under the daily flat there is no overnight or weekend position to gap.
  5. A rounding error at the target. A win sized to close the gap to the target lands a few billionths of a dollar short in floating point; version 6's exact comparison then placed a pointless extra trade (risking $1,500 to make $1). Since version 7 the engine compares with a one-cent tolerance.
  6. Re-entering at once. Checking only whole hours lets a trade close at 12:59 and the next open at 13:00. The engine uses the exit minute and waits at least 10 minutes (10.4).
  7. Clipping a loss at the floor. A position larger than the room above the floor, whose loss is clipped back to the floor after the trade, keeps its full chance of winning while losing less than its stop: version 8 did this for risks under $1 per $100K and for one futures contract above a small room (a $0.50 room, a $1 risk and an $8 win had mean +$0.44 instead of \(-c\)). Since version 9 the floor is a barrier of the trade (4.1).
  8. Fractional positions. A position of exactly \(l/s\), however small, is not a real order. Since version 9 every position is whole lots or contracts (10.1).
  9. Holding a closed position open. Counting a position as open until the next entry is allowed (up to an hour after its exit) lets another account copy a direction that is no longer held, and dates closed profit late. Since version 9 the engine keeps the exit time apart from the next permitted entry.
Chapter 5

Reaching a target before a floor

A phase is passed when the balance gains \(A\) before it loses \(B\): the gambler's ruin. This chapter solves it in an idealised model, then exactly for the plan's actual rule.

5.1 The idealised model: \(P = B/(A+B)\)

Let \(X_n\) be the balance after \(n\) trades, a martingale with \(X_0 = 0\), and \(\tau\) the first time it reaches \(+A\) or \(-B\). If every trade is sized so the balance lands exactly on one of the two, \(X_\tau\) is \(+A\) with probability \(P\) and \(-B\) otherwise, and optional stopping gives

$$0 = \mathbb{E}[X_\tau] = P\,A - (1-P)\,B \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; P = \frac{B}{A+B}.$$

The exact-landing assumption is what this formula needs. Under the plan's rule every failure does end exactly on the floor (5.4), but a pass can end a little above the target (the minimum target distance of 4.1 and the $20 filler margin of 6.3), so the formula is the benchmark and 5.5 gives the exact value.

5.2 With costs, approximately: the Brownian formula

Treat the balance as a Brownian motion with drift \(-c\) and variance \(v = l(w+c)\) per trade. By 2.6, \(e^{\theta X}\) with \(\theta = 2c/v\) is a martingale, and optional stopping gives

Pass probability with costs (Brownian approximation) $$P \approx \frac{e^{\theta B} - 1}{e^{\theta (A+B)} - 1}, \qquad \theta = \frac{2c}{v} = \frac{2\rho}{l\,(w/l + \rho)},\qquad P \approx \frac{B}{A+B}\left(1 - \frac{c\,A}{v}\right).$$

This is accurate when each trade is small next to \(A\) and \(B\) and its size does not change. The plan's trades are capped (40% of the target), shrink near the floor and the daily limit, and jump: the exact chain of 5.5 is used instead, and the Brownian value is shown only for comparison.

5.3 How many trades, approximately

With a constant trade, \((X_n)^2 - n v\) is a martingale before costs, so \(\mathbb{E}[\tau] = \mathbb{E}[X_\tau^2]/v = AB/v\) in the idealised model. With trades that change size the relevant quantity is the accumulated variance \(\sum_n v(X_n)\), not \(v\) times the count; the chain computes the expected count exactly.

5.4 The plan's rule near the floor

Version 6's written rule could leave an account a few dollars above its floor with no trade ever allowed. Version 7 ended the attempt there and bought a replacement; but an account that has not breached is still open, and at firms with a per-person limit (FTMO's $400,000, FXIFY's one account per size) it may still count against it. Since version 8 every account ends in a way the firm records: a breach. At a balance \(x\) (relative to the start), with floor \(-B\), buffer \(b\) ($100 per $100K) and the cost per dollar of risk \(\rho\):

Risk of the next trade $$\text{room}(x) = \frac{x + B - b}{1+\rho}, \qquad l(x) = \min\big(L,\ \text{room}(x),\ \text{today's room},\ \text{margin room}\big) \quad\text{if } \text{room}(x) \ge l_{\min},$$ $$l_{\min} = \max\big(0.1\,L,\ \text{the smallest position's risk}\big).$$
The terminal trade (room(x) < l_min) $$\text{volume: } \frac{x + B}{1+\rho}\ \text{at the plan's stop, rounded down, at least the smallest position};$$ $$\text{its stop order: at the floor itself (the breach level), so a loss ends at } -B \text{ exactly.}$$

(If today's room is smaller than the room to the floor, the trade is a normal one with its stop inside today's room; if that is below the smallest position, the account waits for tomorrow. On futures \(L\) in the first line is at least one contract.)

A normal trade loses at most \(l(1+\rho) \le x + B - b\), so it never reaches the floor. A terminal trade is an ordinary bracket at the plan's reward:risk, small (under a tenth of the normal risk), whose stop sits at the floor: a loss ends the account in a breach, which the firm closes, freeing the allocation; a win continues it, and the account returns to normal trades once the room allows. Where the smallest position at the plan's stop distance would risk more than the room (0.01 lot of gold, one futures contract), its stop order still sits at the floor, so it is closer than the plan's distance; the price reaching the floor ends the account at that moment, even if the price would later have reached the take-profit (version 8 let such a position run to its own stop and clipped the loss afterwards: review of version 8, finding 3). So every failure ends exactly at \(-B\), the text, the engine and the chain follow the same rule, and no account is left open and unused.

5.5 The exact chain

The balance after each trade is a Markov chain on \([-B, A]\), plus a small overshoot above \(A\). At \(x\) the next trade has risk \(l(x)\) as in 5.4, win \(w(x) = \min(\max(\min(k\,l,\ A - x),\ w_{\min},\ \$1),\ \text{cap})\) (4.1), cost \(c(x) = \rho\,l_v(x)\), where \(l_v\) is the risk of the volume traded (\(l\) on a normal trade), and win chance \(p(x) = l_e/(l_e + w + c)\), where \(l_e = l\) on a normal trade and \(l_e = x + B - c\), the distance to the floor, on a terminal trade; it moves to \(x + w\) (a pass if \(x + w \ge A\), possibly above \(A\)) or to \(x - l - c\) (to \(-B\) exactly after a terminal trade). The chain uses unrounded volumes; the engine's whole lots change a trade's risk by less than one volume step. Four functions solve the same recursion with different end values:

One step of the exact chain $$V(x) = p\,V(x+w) + (1-p)\,V(x-l-c), \qquad V = 1 \text{ from } A \text{ up},\ 0 \text{ at } -B,$$ $$N(x) = 1 + p\,N(x+w) + (1-p)\,N(x-l-c), \qquad K(x) = c(x) + p\,K(x+w) + (1-p)\,K(x-l-c),$$ $$T(x) = p\,T(x+w) + (1-p)\,T(x-l-c), \qquad T = \text{the balance where the attempt ends}.$$

\(V\) is the pass probability, \(N\) the expected number of trades, \(K\) the expected total cost and \(T\) the expected final balance. They are solved by value iteration on a grid of about 8,000 points (analytic_v9.py). Because every trade's conditional mean is exactly minus its own cost (4.3), the stopped balance is bounded and the number of trades has finite mean, optional stopping (2.4, 2.5) gives the accounting identity, which the solver checks:

The accounting identity, exact $$P\,\mathbb{E}[X_{\text{end}} \mid \text{pass}] + (1-P)\,\mathbb{E}[X_{\text{end}} \mid \text{fail}] = -K(0), \qquad\text{and with every failure at } -B:\quad P = \frac{B - K(0)}{\mathbb{E}[X_{\text{end}} \mid \text{pass}] + B}.$$

The textbook form \(P = (B - K)/(A+B)\) is the special case where every pass ends exactly at \(A\). Under the plan's rule a pass ends a little above \(A\), so the textbook form overstates \(P\) slightly ([[TEXTBOOK_DIFF]] over the chosen programmes).

5.6 Bold play, and why the plan does not use it

Each trade costs on average \(c = \rho\,l_v\), proportional to the risk of the volume traded, so a phase's expected total cost is \(K = \rho\,\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_i l_i\big]\), the cost rate times the expected total risk staked, not a constant times the number of trades: fewer, larger trades need not cost less. And by 5.5 the pass probability depends on both \(K\) and the mean balance at a pass, \(P = (B - K)/(\mathbb{E}[X_\text{end}\mid\text{pass}] + B)\), so lowering \(K\) while raising the overshoot does not by itself raise \(P\). Version 8 argued from "fewest trades, least cost" to staking enough to reach the target in one win; that step does not follow under variable stakes and overshoots (review of version 8, finding 18). Bold play, which Dubins and Savage (1965) proved optimal for reaching a goal in a fair or slightly unfavourable game of fixed odds and exact landing, is a theorem about that model, not about this one. What is measured is that version 6's search, left free, moved every programme to \(k\) of 20–30 and payout cycles of 25–30% of the account, so that a single trade could pass a phase or fill a payout; that is an empirical result for its grid, not a proof of optimality.

Version 7 gave that up, deliberately, and versions 8 and 9 keep it so. Several firms forbid exactly this behaviour: Fintokei forbids reaching a target "in one or a few identical trades", The5ers bans "one-trade target attainment" permanently, Alpha forbids "all or nothing" trading, FundingPips' Profit Concentration Policy reacts to one trade idea above 60% of a target, and FundedNext, Hola Prime, FTMO and FXIFY reserve the right to treat concentrated outcomes as gambling. The plan therefore caps every trade and every day at 40% of the current target (6.7), so that every phase takes at least three winning days (a payout taken on its date before the target can rest on fewer, 6.7). Under that cap one win can never end a phase, the product formula for bold play (the chance of passing as a sequence of all-or-nothing trades) never applies, and the chain of 5.5 is the exact value in its model. The settings within the policy are chosen by search (Chapter 9), and the search's winners are empirical results for the grid searched.

For reference, the bold product formula, \(P = \sum_j p_j \prod_{i<j}(1-p_i)\) with \(x_{j+1} = x_j - l_j - c_j\), is exact only if every possible win ends the phase, i.e. \(k\,l(x_j) \ge A - x_j\) at every state on the all-loss path; \(k L \ge A + B\) is not enough, because \(l(x)\) shrinks near the floor. Version 6's tables printed wins of \(A - x_j\) even where \(k\,l(x_j)\) was smaller; those rows described trades the plan would not place.

Chapter 6

The evaluation in full

6.1 Two phases

Always, \(\Pr(\text{pass both}) = P_1 \cdot \Pr(\text{pass 2} \mid \text{passed 1})\). A fresh second account resets the balance, but not the calendar: the weekday and hour at which phase 2 starts, and on futures the price level that sets a contract's risk, depend on how and when phase 1 ended (and on the review that follows it), and the daily flat and the calendar shape the path phase 2 can trade. Independent future price increments do not make that starting state independent. So the conditional probability is not in general an unconditional \(P_2\) (version 8 said it was: review of version 8, finding 13). The engine simulates the two phases in sequence on one path, so its pass rate estimates \(P_1\Pr(2\mid1)\) directly, with \(\Pr(2\mid1)\) taken under the start states phase 1 actually leads to. In the calendar-free chain of 5.5, where a phase depends on nothing but the balance, the phases are independent and \(P = P_1P_2\) is exact; that is the only model in which this document multiplies unconditional probabilities.

Version 6 also said that putting the larger target first saves time and cost. It does not in general: with the Brownian trade count of 5.3 and targets \(A, C\) over a common \(B\), the expected number of trades is \(AB/v + \frac{B}{A+B}\cdot CB/v\), which for \(A = 10{,}000\), \(C = 5{,}000\), \(B = 10{,}000\) and \(v = 1{,}500^2 \times 5\) is 11.11 trades, against 10.37 with the targets reversed. The claim is withdrawn.

6.2 The daily loss limit

Most firms add a floor that is reset every day: the account may not fall more than \(d\) below a reference level fixed at the reset. Firms fix that level differently:

[[DLL_TABLE]]

Two things differ by firm: the reference fixed at the reset (balance, equity, the higher of the two, the previous day's close) and the base of the percentage. At FTMO, FundedNext, BrightFunded and Blue Guardian the allowance is a fixed share of the initial balance, subtracted from the day's reference. At FundingPips, The5ers, FXIFY, GFT, Hola Prime, Maven, Alpha Capital and Fintokei it is the same share of the day's reference itself: The5ers' own example is a $100,000 account at $110,000 of equity at the reset, whose loss allowance for the day becomes $5,500; a FundingPips account starting a day at $94,000 has $3,760, not $4,000. FunderPro says only that its 5% is balance-based at the start of the day; the engine takes the stricter of the two bases there. Version 7 used the initial balance at every firm except Fintokei, and its response said that being flat at the reset made the engine's limit the firm's. Being flat makes balance and equity equal at the reset; it does not change the base. Version 8 recomputes each firm's allowance at every reset with its own base (acct_mc._dll_at).

With an open position at the reset, an equity-based or higher-of reference rises with the open profit, and a smooth reversal can then break the day's limit with no gap at all. The plan is flat at every reset (6.5), so the reference is the balance. Within the day, every trade's risk plus its cost fits the room left (5.4); after two or three losses the account waits for the next day.

6.3 Minimum trading days: the joint condition

FTMO (4), FundedNext (5), BrightFunded (5), FXIFY Classic (4), FunderPro Classic (4), Alpha (3), Hola Prime (3) and Fintokei (3) require days with at least one trade in each phase; Fintokei also before every payout, and Alpha five before its first. A phase is passed when the target is met and the days are complete. Since the 40% concentration policy (6.7) caps every day at 40% of the target, a phase needs at least three winning days, so the three-day minimums are always met; the four- and five-day ones may not be. How a day counts differs: FTMO counts "any day ... during which at least one position is opened", BrightFunded a day on which a trade was open for at least 60 seconds, Fintokei an executed trade "with a real market outcome", Alpha a trade opened and closed on the day.

Filler trades. Where the target is reached with days missing, the plan stops trading the strategy and, on each of the next trading days not yet traded (found on the market's real calendar), opens one minimal position: 0.01 lot (one contract on futures), at the day's first permitted hour, with an emergency stop at the account's breach level, closed at the market after 3 minutes. It is a real trade in the engine: its cost and result enter the balance, it can lose, and the day counts only if the firm's definition is met (at BrightFunded, held 60 seconds). The engine trades to the firm's target plus $20 per $100K, so a filler's few cents or dollars rarely matter; if a loss takes the balance under the firm's target, ordinary trading resumes. Version 8 counted the missing days on the calendar without trading them (review of version 8, finding 5). The firms' day definitions state no minimum volume; that such a trade is accepted is an assumption of the model, and Chapter [[CH_RISK]].8 measures the plan without it.

No fillers at Fintokei and Alpha Capital. Fintokei's all-in article objects to "much smaller trades ... just to meet the minimum 3-day trading requirement", and Alpha does not allow minimal lots to pass trading days on a qualified account. There the plan places no filler trades: a phase never needs one (three winning days), and a funded cycle short of its days (Fintokei's 3 per payout, Alpha's 5 before the first) collects them with ordinary trades of the plan, at most one a day. Fillers are not used where a rule counts profitable days (6.4).

6.4 Profitable-day rules

The5ers, Maven, Blue Guardian, GFT (whose days count only with +0.5%) and FundingPips' 95% option require days with at least +0.5% profit in each phase; FundingPips' 95% option, GFT (4), Hola Prime, Maven and, in its stricter reading, Blue Guardian also in each payout cycle. Hola Prime's bi-weekly option needs its three "within a 14-day period": the engine counts the profitable days within the 14 calendar days ending on the request day, so older ones expire (version 8 counted every profitable day since the last payout: review of version 8, finding 9). (Version 7 also applied +0.5% to Fintokei, whose rule counts any trading day.) While such days are missing, the plan trades normally until the day's profit reaches

$$g_{\text{today}} = \max\!\left(0.55\%\,S,\; \frac{\text{distance left to the target}}{\text{profitable days still needed}}\right)$$

and then stops for the day; every trade is a full fair bracket whose take-profit is capped at what is left of today's target (but never closer than the minimum distance of 4.1). Stopping for the day is a stopping time, so in a cost-free fair game the expected stopped balance is unchanged; that alone does not keep the pass probability unchanged, which by 5.5 is \(B/(B + \mathbb{E}[X_\text{end}\mid\text{pass}])\) and moves with the mean balance at a pass (a fair game ending at +1 or −1 passes with probability 1/2, one ending at +2 or −1 with 1/3). The pass probability is invariant only where every pass lands exactly on the target and every failure on the floor; under the plan's overshoots it is not, and with costs every extra trade costs \(c\) as well (review of version 8, finding 19). The engine measures their effect in every programme's chapter. Under Maven's funded ceiling (profit kept at or below $4,900, 7.4) the plan also keeps room for the days still needed: every win leaves 0.55% of the account below the ceiling for each profitable day still needed after today, and for today as well if the win would leave today short of +0.5%; otherwise it is cut, or the plan waits for tomorrow. Without that reserve an account can reach the ceiling a profitable day short and never be paid (Appendix A, n10).

6.5 The daily flat, weekends and news

The plan opens no position from 19:00 UTC and closes every position at the 20:00 UTC open, every weekday. Every daily reset in the plan falls between 20:00 and 00:00 UTC (FTMO and BrightFunded near midnight Prague time, GFT and Blue Guardian at 5 pm New York, Fintokei and Maven at 00:00 UTC), so the account is flat at each of them. This also removes overnight financing, overnight and weekend gaps, and the widened spreads of the daily break. The cost is time: a trade still open at 20:00 is closed between its stop and its target (a fair outcome) and the next one waits until the next day's open. Such a trade is decided at that open, with that day's loss allowance and caps: every daily figure in the engine (loss, profit, profitable and trading days) belongs to the day a trade opens, which under the daily flat is also the day it closes, and a payout requested during a day does not restart that day's caps. A position opens at the first hourly open that has not begun before the decision (Appendix A, n17).

News. Restricted news windows differ by firm (Maven: no opening or closing within 2 minutes of a red-folder release, in evaluations too; FundingPips: profits from 5 minutes before to 5 minutes after may be deducted; FTMO funded: no new orders within 2 minutes; FundedNext: 40% of news profit counted on funded accounts). A bracket opened earlier can still reach its stop or target inside a window. The plan closes positions before any release that a firm restricts for the account's stage and market (US, euro-area and Japanese releases alike) and opens nothing until the window has passed. The engine has no news calendar, so the headline is a model without news restrictions. Chapter [[CH_LOCK]] runs a stylised news-hours sensitivity in which every account is flat from 12:00 to 14:00 UTC (and 08:00 to 09:00 on EURUSD) every weekday; it is a scenario, not a bound: those hours do not contain every restricted release, and removing trading hours has no proven one-way effect on cash per month (it changes which trades happen, when days qualify and when payouts fall).

6.6 Review time

Firms take one to three working days to check a passed phase and issue the next account, and about a working day to process a payout. The engine counts both in working days (Monday to Friday) on the path's calendar: one after phase 1, the firm's stated time after phase 2, so a three-day review that starts on a Friday afternoon ends on the next Wednesday afternoon, and a payout requested on a Friday is received on Monday. Version 8 added 24 hours per working day (review of version 8, finding 20).

6.7 The concentration policy

On every account the plan caps the win of every trade at 40% of the current target (the phase target in an evaluation, the payout target on a funded account), and stops for the day once the day's profit reaches 40% of that target. Three consequences follow:

  • every phase needs at least three winning days, so no pass rests on one trade or one day. A funded cycle that reaches its target needs three too, but a payout taken on its date before the target can rest on fewer: where a firm requires profitable days per payout, that is a rule of the programme (6.4), not a consequence of the cap;
  • FundingPips' Profit Concentration Policy (60% of a phase target from one trade idea) can never trigger, and FXIFY's 25% best-day rule is met by its own tighter cap;
  • the effective reward-to-risk is capped: in phase 2 of a 5% target on 100K a win is at most $2,000, so at 1.75% risk at most 1.14 : 1. The search (Chapter 9) therefore covers smaller \(k\) and the risk per trade.

Chapter 9 measures what the policy costs against the uncapped strategy of version 6, programme by programme.
