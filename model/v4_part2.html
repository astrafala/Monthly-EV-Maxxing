Part 2

Execution

The plan that maximised expected value per month per person in Part 1, written step by step and checked against every firm's published rules (Chapter 23). It uses one position at a time on the Nasdaq, grows by adding firms rather than risk, and by default runs one account per firm.

Read this first

24.2 Every trade

  1. One position at a time per account; the next one as soon as the last closes.
  2. Window: Monday to Friday, 01:00–20:00 UTC. No new trade after 19:00 UTC Friday; be flat by 20:30 UTC Friday on every account.
  3. Direction: the shared rule (26.3). Nasdaq accounts never oppose each other; the gold account follows its own rule.
  4. Risk = the smallest of 1.5% of the account size, (room above the floor − 0.1%), and (today's loss room − 0.2%).
  5. Stop = 0.75 × the hourly move, attached on entry, never moved.
  6. Target = 5 × the stop distance, capped at the distance left to the phase or cycle target plus this trade's cost, and at any daily profit target or cap (26.4).
  7. News: no new trade from 15 minutes before to 5 minutes after a high-impact US release (gold: also US releases). On funded accounts, be flat 10 minutes before.

24.3 Every account

  • Never abandon a live account (Section 3.3). At a phase target, stop; fill missing minimum days with one small trade a day where the firm counts trading days (FTMO, FundedNext, Alpha, BrightFunded, Blue Guardian).
  • Where profitable days are required (The5ers, Maven, FundingPips 95%), never construct them with tiny trades; use the daily profit target of 26.4.
  • Funded: trade each cycle to its target, stop, request the payout on the first allowed day, withdraw all profit.

25.3 Measure costs and the hourly move

  1. On each firm's demo, write down the value of one point per lot (V) for the Nasdaq (and gold for FundedNext), and the minimum and maximum lot size per order.
  2. Average the spread over a US afternoon (14:00–20:00 UTC) and add about 0.6 points for slippage. If the Nasdaq total is above about 3 points, expect a third less value at that firm.
Hourly move (simple version)hourly move ≈ average of |close − previous close| over the last 100 hourly bars × 1.25 (recompute every Monday)

The trade, step by step

26.1 The formulas (any account size S)

Riskroom = balance − floor    day room = daily limit − (start-of-day balance − current equity)
risk = min(1.5% × S, room − 0.1% × S, day room − 0.2% × S)
Stop, lots, coststop points = 0.75 × hourly move    lots = risk ÷ (stop points × V)    cost $ ≈ (spread + 0.6) × lots × V
Targetto go = phase (or cycle) target balance − balance
target $ = min(5 × risk, to go + cost $ + $5, today's remaining daily target or cap)    target points = target $ ÷ (lots × V)

If the lots exceed a firm's maximum per order, place the position as two orders at the same moment with the same stop and target, or reduce the risk to fit.

26.3 Direction

Shared direction (Nasdaq accounts)If any of your accounts holds a Nasdaq position: same direction as that position.
Otherwise: long if the last completed hourly close is above the close 5 hours earlier, short if it is below.

The gold account (FundedNext) uses the same 5-hour rule on gold, by itself. Gold and the Nasdaq are different markets, so its positions are neither copies of nor hedges against the others. The rule is chosen for being simple and common, not because it did best in Chapter 22's sample.

26.4 Daily profit targets and caps

  • Profitable days required (The5ers and Maven in each phase; FundingPips 95% in each funded cycle; a profitable day is at least +0.5%): until enough profitable days are done, each day's profit target is the larger of 0.55% and (distance left ÷ profitable days still needed). Stop for the day when it is reached. Risk stays 1.5% on every trade.
  • Best-day rules (Maven funded after $5,000 of payouts: 50%): cap the take-profit so that no day exceeds the limit.
  • GFT funded: at most $3,000 profit a day; the take-profit never exceeds what is left of it.

The rules that apply everywhere

  • No opposite positions in correlated markets across any of your accounts, at any firm, at any time.
  • Everything in your own name; no third-party account management; no signals or copy services from others.
  • Stay under each firm's cap, and within its rules on how many accounts may carry the same trades.
  • No exploiting platform errors, latency or price feeds; no tick scalping; no trades shorter than a minute.
  • Minimum trading days may be met with small, real trades; profitable days may not be manufactured.
  • Respect each firm's news windows and weekend rules on funded accounts (the plan is flat for both).
Chapter 28

Running many accounts: the daily routine

28.1 The work

{{WORK_TABLE}}

At 1 : 5 a full-size trade lasts about four to eight hours including waiting. Nasdaq accounts usually enter together, so the day has a handful of entry moments, each with an order ticket per free account, sized for that account's own balance.

28.2 What a program does on each account

StepAction
1If this account has a position open, do nothing until it closes.
2Outside 01:00–20:00 UTC Monday–Friday, after 19:00 UTC Friday, or within the news window: wait.
3Read balance, start-of-day balance, the current phase or cycle target and any daily target or cap. If a target is reached, stop.
4Compute risk (26.1). If the day room is below 0.3% of the account, wait for the next day.
5Read the shared direction (26.3) from a file that all your programs read.
6Compute stop, lots, cost and target. Send a market order with both attached.
7Log: time, account, balance, risk, lots, stop, target, direction, fill, result, cost.
8Friday 20:30 UTC: close any open position. Funded: close 10 minutes before a listed release.

Run each program on a free demo for two weeks first. Where a firm requires approval for programs (The5ers) or copying (FXIFY), get it in writing first, or trade by hand.

Chapter 29

Funded accounts and payouts

29.1 Cycles of the full allowance

On every funded account, trade the normal plan with the cycle target at the allowance (+10%; +12% at FundingPips Flex; +8% at Maven; +7% for GFT's first cycle). When it is reached, stop; request a payout of all profit on the first allowed day. About one cycle in two ends at the target and one in two ends the account: that is expected. Half of funded accounts end before their first payout, and then the fee is not refunded either; that is the price of the faster cycle. A first cycle of only +$1,000 makes the first payout and refund far more likely (91%) at a cost of about a sixth of the value per month (Section 19.3).

31.5 Tax

Payouts are usually taxable income; fees are often deductible. Keep one ledger of every fee and payout with dates and currencies, and check your country's rules with an adviser.

Chapter 32

Final checklist

  • I am 18 or older and hold, in cash I can lose, the bad-case cash low point for the version I will run.
  • I have read each firm's current objectives, prohibited practices, payout terms and cap, and confirmed every "check" item in writing.
  • I measured Nasdaq (and gold) costs and the point value at every firm.
  • My programs or routine follow Chapter 24 exactly and passed two weeks on demo; approvals obtained where required.
  • One position per account; risk 1.5%; stop and target attached on entry; 1 : 5.
  • Nasdaq accounts share one direction; no account ever opposes another; FundedNext trades gold on its own.
  • Only one account at GFT, FunderPro, FundedNext, FXIFY, The5ers and Maven.
  • Daily guard, news rule, Friday close, daily profit targets and caps are in place.
  • Everything is in my own name; I do not share, sell or copy this plan to or from anyone.
  • I understand that about seven attempts in ten lose the fee, that the first month is usually negative, and that firms may refuse payouts or change rules.

The whole document in six lines

A zero-skill trade is a fair bet, so a phase passes with probability B ÷ (A + B). A funded account pays out, on average, the split times its loss allowance. Time is set by the instrument's cost per unit of movement, the stop, the reward-to-risk ratio and the firm's calendar. The best plan found is one Nasdaq position at a time, stop 0.75 hourly moves, 1 : 5, risk 1.5%, funded cycles of the full allowance. One account at each of eleven firms is worth about {{HEAD_ONE}} a month in theory; the full caps about {{HEAD_FULL}}, with far more failed accounts per firm. The firms' discretion is the one thing the maths cannot price.
