16 prop firms · zero-skill valuation

Prop Firm Convexity Audit

You asked whether the "FTMO Convex Edge" strategy works. The idea behind it is sound and the edge is larger than the PDFs claim. The PDFs' maths is mostly wrong, and the protocol they recommend loses money on real data. Whether any of it pays depends on the firm choosing to pay, and no model can price that.

Rules as published Sept–Oct 2026 · Real-data test: EURUSD hourly bars, 18 Dec 2023 – 2 Oct 2026 · 37 account types modelled · Prices in USD (EUR at 1.17)

Verdict
  • The convexity is real. A trader with zero skill has positive expected value against most prop-firm contracts, provided the firm pays. FTMO 100K 2-Step: about +$2,100 per attempt in the model and +$1,760 ± $160 on real EURUSD data, against a €540 fee.
  • The PDFs give the wrong reason. The value does not come from Jensen's inequality or from "maximising variance". The firm hands you a loss budget (the max drawdown) that it never charges you for. At zero drift, every dollar of that budget eventually gets paid out at the profit split.
  • The PDF protocol loses money. Implemented as written on real data it returns −$173 ± $79 per attempt. The "quit after two full-size losses" rule and fixed 1:1 sizing throw away most of the option.
  • Hedging between firms adds no expected value. Expected value is additive, so hedging only changes the spread of outcomes: it turns a lottery that loses the fee about 73% of the time into a near-certain cash flow. FTMO's terms ban it explicitly, including against accounts at other firms.
  • The real risk sits with the counterparty. Payouts are discretionary "rewards" on simulated accounts, the optimal play is what the conduct clauses target, and 80–100 firms closed in 2024–25. On paper FTMO stays break-even even if 77% of payouts are denied, but denials come with bans, so the real limit is how many attempts you get.
Part 1 · From zero

What a challenge is, mathematically

Treat the account balance as a process you control. Each day you place one bracket: take-profit w, stop l. With no predictive skill the mid-price is a martingale, so the win probability is l/(w+l) and the expected P&L of a bracket is exactly minus its costs. A challenge pays you when the balance reaches a target A before it hits a floor B below the start. A funded account pays you a share of every withdrawal until it hits the floor. Everything below follows from optional stopping (a fair game stopped at a sensible time is still fair).

1. Pass probability, static floor FTMO 2-Step, most CFD firms

P(pass) = B / (A + B) exact, any sizing, any RR, no deadline P(pass) = (B + O_floor) / (A + O_target + B) with overshoot past either barrier

Neither sizing nor reward-to-risk changes this, and neither does variance. Overshoot does. Overshooting the target wastes probability. Overshooting the floor is free, because the firm absorbs it. The optimal play is bold play: size every bracket so the balance lands exactly on the target or exactly on the floor (or the daily loss limit), in as few trades as possible. FTMO Phase 1 gives 50%, Phase 2 gives 67%, so 33% reach funded. The PDF's fixed 1:1 bracket at $4,800 needs $14,400 to clear a $10,000 target, which cuts Phase 1 from 50% to about 41%.

2. End-of-day trailing drawdown Topstep, Apex EOD, Tradeify, Lucid, FTMO 1-Step

An end-of-day trail only moves when a day closes at a new high. If you can reach the target in one day, the trail never moves and you get the static answer B/(A+B). Firms add consistency rules to block this. A best-day cap forces several profitable days, and each one lifts the floor. That is where EOD accounts lose value (dynamic programming, $3k target, $2k trail):

3. Intraday trailing drawdown Apex intraday, MFFU Rapid, TPT PRO funded, Bulenox opt. 1

P(reach A before a drawdown of D from the equity peak) = exp(−A / D)

This is the maximum over every strategy when prices move continuously. For a driftless process, the peak reached before a drawdown of D is exponentially distributed with mean D, and position sizing only changes the clock. Intraday trailing is the rule that destroys most of the convexity. It is the firms' strongest defence.

4. What a funded account is worth the result the PDFs needed

E[total gross withdrawals] = E[starting balance − final balance] + E[trading P&L] ≤ D − E[costs] (zero drift, run to the floor) Funded value to you ≈ split × D × (1 − 2κ) + refund × P(first payout)

This is accounting, not a probability argument. Money paid out has to come either from trading profits or from the balance falling. With zero edge the first term is zero, so the firm's expected payout equals the drawdown it lets you use. FTMO 100K: 0.80 × $10,000 = $8,000, whatever reward-to-risk you trade, plus the fee refund. Lock levels, payout caps (Apex closes the account after six payouts), "50% of balance" rules and intraday trails all reduce the usable budget.

5. Costs

A bracket that resolves within a day costs about κ·√(w·l), where κ is the cost-to-risk ratio of a 1:1 trade with a stop of one daily sigma. On EURUSD (0.75 pip all-in against roughly a 55-pip day) κ ≈ 1.4%. Total cost scales like κ·AB/L for bet size L. Few large trades cost little and many small trades cost a lot. Scalping is how ordinary traders lose: 10-pip stops make κ around 7.5% per trade.

EV per attempt ≈ −fee + Π P(pass phase i) × ( split·D·(1 − 2κ) − activation fee + refund·q )

What the ranking says

  • Static 10% drawdown 2-Steps lead in dollars (FundedNext, FundingPips, The5ers, Alpha Pro 10%, FTMO: +$2,000 to +$2,500). Their contracts are linear, which also makes them the easiest to hedge.
  • FTMO 1-Step comes out top (+$3,465) because its trailing loss resets with every reward and the split is 90%. Its 50% best-day rule costs only a few points.
  • Futures evaluations return the most per dollar of evaluation fee (Apex EOD 9×, Topstep 7.7×) because they are cheap, but they are worth only $300–$550 each. Activation fees, locks at start + $100 and payout ceilings cap the funded value near $1,500 on a $2,000 budget.
  • Intraday trailing accounts are close to worthless even with perfect play: Apex intraday +$92, Bulenox option 1 +$31, TPT PRO about $0, MFFU Rapid −$26.
  • 3-step programmes are a trap. Three B/(A+B) factors multiply: Alpha Three −$39, Maven 3-Step +$14.
Part 5 · Hedging

Hedging between prop firms

Expected value is linear. The expected total of several accounts equals the sum of their expected values, however they are correlated. A hedge cannot create value. If each account is worth +$2,000 on its own, a hedged book is worth exactly the sum. If each is negative, hedging locks in the loss. What a hedge changes is the shape of the outcome.

On static-floor accounts that shape becomes flat, because every stage's value is a straight line in its own balance. FTMO 100K, zero costs:

Phase 1 at start$2,867slope 0.287 per $
Phase 2 at start$5,735slope 0.573 per $
Funded at start$8,602slope 0.80 per $
Net of €540 fee+$2,235per attempt

A constant opposite position equal to the slope makes the total value constant, and the outcome becomes deterministic. Three versions:

  • Two identical Phase-1 accounts, opposite positions. Target and floor are both $10,000, so exactly one passes every time. Two fees always buy one Phase-2 account, with no personal capital at risk.
  • Different firms. Size the positions in the ratio of the slopes (FTMO Phase 1 at 0.287 against FundedNext Phase 1 at 0.316 means 1 : 0.91), then rebalance whenever an account crosses a barrier.
  • Your own broker account. Hold 0.287× the opposite exposure during Phase 1, 0.573× during Phase 2 and 0.80× once funded. You lock in about +$2,235 per attempt minus costs on both legs. The hedge leg needs about $2,900 per challenge account and $8,000 per funded account to absorb its worst move.

Hedging cannot fix a refused payout. In a hedge against your own account, the paths where the prop account wins are exactly the paths where your real account lost. If that payout is denied, the loss is real money, up to $8,000 per funded account, where the unhedged loss would have been the fee. Across firms, a denial forfeits the option value of the pair. FTMO's forbidden-practices page bans "opposite positions" across accounts, including "accounts held with various operators/providers", and the listed consequence is forfeiture of rewards and termination of all agreements. Other firms have similar clauses. So in real life, cross-firm hedging turns a positive-EV lottery into a bet on not being detected. That probability is not something this analysis can estimate.

Part 6 · Real life

Why firms survive a contract that is positive EV for a coin flip

  • The typical customer is far worse than random. A coin-flipper with wide stops passes FTMO Phase 1 about 46% of the time. Industry estimates put real pass rates near 10–15%, mostly because high-turnover trading pays κ hundreds of times.
  • Rules remove convexity where it is cheapest to remove: intraday trailing drawdowns, best-day consistency rules, activation fees, locks at start + $100, payout ladders, six-payout ceilings and "max 50% of balance" withdrawals.
  • Conduct clauses target the optimal play. FTMO lists "overleveraging, overexposure, one-sided bets, account rolling" and cross-account hedging. Bold play (5% risk, one trade a day, aimed at the target) is exactly that pattern. The 1%-risk version still clears +$1,150 on real data and looks like ordinary trading, though it is still the same zero-skill bet underneath.
  • Payouts are discretionary. Funded accounts are simulated, and payouts are "rewards" the firm may refuse under its terms.
  • Firms fail. True Forex Funds closed in May 2024 owing about $1.2M. The Funded Trader admitted to more than $2M in denied payouts. MyForexFunds was shut down by a CFTC action in 2023; the case later collapsed, and pre-2023 customers were never made whole. Roughly 80–100 firms closed in 2024–25, many after MetaQuotes revoked their platform licences.
  • Hard caps limit scale. One verified identity per person, $400K allocation at FTMO, 5 funded accounts at Topstep, 20 at Apex. Using other people's identities is fraud under every firm's terms.
Method & sources

How these numbers were made

Evaluation phases are solved by value iteration over (balance, end-of-day high) states with two-point daily brackets, a daily loss limit, best-day caps and a cost drift of κ·√(wl). Intraday trails use the exponential-peak result. Funded values use the withdrawal identity with lock levels and payout caps. Real-data runs use yfinance hourly EURUSD. Rule sets are mid-2026 snapshots and change often, especially promo prices. Rows marked † have a phase-2 target or funded terms inferred rather than published.
