Part 2

Execution

The plan that maximised expected value per month per person in Part 1, written step by step. It stays inside every firm's published rules and FTMO's 1–1.5% risk guidance. It uses one position at a time on the Nasdaq and grows by adding accounts and firms, never by adding risk.

Chapter 23

The plan on one page

Read this first

  • All figures assume zero skill and that every firm pays every payout. Payouts are discretionary; one refusal usually ends that firm.
  • The trades have no edge. About seven attempts in ten lose their whole fee; one slot is ahead after a month about one time in five, after a year about 96% (Chapter 29).

23.1 Accounts

  1. Start with FTMO 2-Step: four 100K accounts (or two 200K), the $400,000 cap.
  2. After the first payouts arrive, add firms one at a time (Section 21.5): FundingPips 2-Step (4 × 100K), The5ers High Stakes (1 × 100K), FundedNext Stellar 2-Step (3 × 100K), Alpha Capital Pro 10% (3 × 100K). Add FXIFY and GFT only after their caps are confirmed in writing (Chapter 26).
  3. Everything in your own name, one registration per firm. Never trade for or with anyone else.

23.2 Every trade, on every account

  1. Instrument: Nasdaq 100 only (US100 / NAS100 CFD; micro Nasdaq futures, MNQ, at a futures firm).
  2. One position at a time per account; the next one as soon as the last closes.
  3. Window: Monday to Friday, 01:00–20:00 UTC. No new trade after 19:00 UTC Friday; be flat by 20:30 UTC Friday.
  4. Direction: the shared rule (Section 25.3). If any of your accounts already holds a Nasdaq position, a new position takes the same direction. No account ever opposes another.
  5. Risk = the smallest of 1.5% of the account size, (room above the floor − 0.1%), and (today's loss room − 0.2%).
  6. Stop = 0.75 × the hourly move (about 60–65 Nasdaq points in 2024–26), attached on entry and never moved.
  7. Target = 5 × the stop distance; but if 5 × risk would pass the phase or cycle target, the take-profit is the distance left plus this trade's cost.
  8. News: no new trade from 15 minutes before to 5 minutes after a high-impact US release. On funded accounts, be flat 10 minutes before it.

23.3 Every account

  • Never abandon a live account (Section 3.3). At a phase target, stop; fill missing minimum days with one tiny trade a day.
  • Funded: each cycle, trade until +10% (or the account ends), then stop. Request the payout on the first eligible day and withdraw all profit (Chapter 28).
  • Respect each firm's extra rules (Chapter 26).
Chapter 24

Before you start

24.1 Requirements

  • Adult (18+), and each firm's identity check in your own name.
  • Money you can lose entirely, never borrowed. Because all accounts move together, the cash low point scales with the number of slots (Section 29.1): about $15,000 typical and $55,000 in a bad case (1 in 20) for four FTMO slots; about $77,000 typical and $165,000 bad for 23 slots.
  • A platform per firm (MetaTrader 5, cTrader, DXtrade or the firm's own; NinjaTrader or Tradovate for futures), and either an automated program or several hours a day to place trades (Chapter 27).
  • Each firm's current objectives, forbidden practices and payout terms, read in full and saved as PDF with the date.

24.2 Measure your costs at each firm

The plan rests on the Nasdaq costing about 2 points per round trip including slippage. At each firm, on a free demo:

  1. Open the symbol specification. Write down the value of one point for one lot (V) and the minimum lot size.
  2. During 14:00–20:00 UTC, note the spread every few minutes for a day and average it.
  3. Add 0.5–0.6 points for slippage. If the total is above about 3 points, expect about a third less value at that firm; consider leaving it out.

24.3 Measure the hourly move

Hourly move (simple version)hourly move ≈ average of |close − previous close| over the last 100 hourly bars × 1.25

Recompute it every Monday and use the same value on every account for the week.

24.4 Copying trades between accounts

  • Within one firm: use only a copying method the firm allows. FTMO allows copying between your own accounts within the cap. FundingPips allows copying between your own FundingPips accounts, but forbids inbound copying from signal providers or copier services. Check every other firm in writing.
  • Across firms: place each firm's orders yourself, or run a separate program on each account that applies the same written rules. That is one trading plan applied independently on each account, not a copy service.
  • Never copy trades from or to another person.

Win chance is the fair value, stop ÷ (stop + target), before costs. Leverage does not bind: 23 lots at 31,000 is $0.7 million notional, far below 1:50 on 100K.

25.3 The direction rule

Shared directionIf any of your accounts holds a Nasdaq position: same direction as that position.
Otherwise: long if the last completed hourly close is above the close 5 hours earlier, short if it is below.

This is a standard short-term momentum rule, chosen because it is simple and common, not because it did best in Chapter 22's sample (choosing it for that reason would be fitting noise). It is not expected to have an edge. Its purpose is to be a written, repeatable plan, applied identically everywhere, that never opposes itself.

25.4 Futures accounts (Topstep 50K)

Micro Nasdaq futures pay $2 per point. Risk about $950 per trade; stop 0.75 hourly moves (about 50 points at 25,000) is about $100 per contract, so about 9 contracts, well under the 50-micro limit. Use 1 : 3 here, not 1 : 5: the Combine's best-day rule ($1,500, half the $3,000 target) caps a single day's win, and in simulation 1 : 3 did best. Funded (Express) accounts: stop for the day once the day is a qualifying winning day ($150+), and request the maximum allowed payout as soon as five winning days are in.

The rules that apply everywhere

  • No opposite positions across any of your accounts, at any firm, at any time.

Chapter 27

Running many accounts: the daily routine

27.1 The work

At 1 : 5 a full-size trade lasts about four to eight hours including waiting, so each account takes one to three new trades a day. Accounts in the same direction usually open together. With 23 accounts that is 25–70 order tickets a day by hand, each sized for its own balance, or one program per account.

27.2 What a program does on each account

StepAction
1If this account has a position open, do nothing until it closes.
2Outside 01:00–20:00 UTC Monday–Friday, after 19:00 UTC Friday, or within the news window: wait.
3Read balance, start-of-day balance and the current phase or cycle target. If the target is reached, stop.
4Compute risk (25.1). If the day room is below 0.3% of the account, wait for the next day.
5Read the shared direction (25.3) from a common file or service that all your programs read.
6Compute stop, lots, cost and target. Send a market order with both attached.
7Log: time, account, balance, risk, lots, stop, target, direction, fill, result, cost.
8Friday 20:30 UTC: close any open position. Funded: close 10 minutes before a listed release.

Check each firm's terms on automated trading before running a program there; FTMO permits Expert Advisors, Apex does not allow automation. Run every program on a free demo for two weeks first: both orders attached, sizes as in 25.2, the daily guard, the news rule and the Friday close all firing.

27.3 Weekly

  • Monday: update the hourly move; check every firm's announcements for rule changes.
  • Reconcile the log with each platform's statements; record every fee and payout.
  • Run the checks of Section 29.3.
Chapter 28

Funded accounts and payouts

28.1 Cycles of +10%

On every funded CFD account, trade the normal plan with the cycle target at +10% of the account size. When it is reached, stop; request a payout of all profit on the first allowed day. The balance returns to the start, the floor stays, and the next +10% cycle begins. GFT: aim for +7% in the first cycle (its first two payouts are capped at 6%).

About one cycle in two ends at +10% and one in two ends the account; that is expected. The total paid averages the split times the allowance (Chapter 5); +10% cycles collect it fastest.

Chapter 29

Month by month: cash, odds and when to stop

29.1 What to expect

{{MONTHS_TABLE}}

The first month is almost always negative: fees are paid at once, payouts come four weeks later. The low point is usually in weeks three to six. Because the accounts move together, more slots multiply the swing as well as the expected value.

29.2 Bankroll rules

  • Hold the "bad case" low point of Section 29.1 for the slots you run, in cash you can lose.
  • Add firms only from profits already received, in the order of Section 21.5.
  • Withdraw payouts to a separate account. Only the planned budget buys new attempts.
  • Fix in advance how many months you will run before reviewing.

29.4 Tax

Payouts are usually taxable income; fees are often deductible. With several firms, keep a single ledger of every fee and payout with dates and currencies, and check your country's rules with an adviser.

Final checklist

  • I have read each firm's current objectives, forbidden practices, payout terms and cap, and confirmed the cap in writing where needed.
  • I measured Nasdaq costs (≤ 3 points round trip) and the point value at every firm.
  • My program or routine follows Chapter 23 exactly and passed two weeks on demo.

The whole document in six lines

A zero-skill trade is a fair bet, so a phase passes with probability B ÷ (A + B). A funded account pays out, on average, the split times its loss allowance. Time is set by the instrument's cost per unit of movement, the stop, the reward-to-risk ratio and the firm's calendar. The best plan found is one Nasdaq position at a time, stop 0.75 hourly moves, 1 : 5, risk 1.5%, funded cycles of +10%. That gives about {{HEAD_SLOT}} per month per 100K slot and, across the caps of seven CFD firms, about {{HEAD_PERSON}} per month per person in theory. More positions per account do not help; more accounts do. The firms' discretion is the one thing the maths cannot price.
